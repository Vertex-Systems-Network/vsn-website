Build options

From managed platforms to custom full-stack engineering.

We map the required features and constraints to a platform before recommending a stack. The final proposal lists the chosen platform, included features, third-party services, delivery responsibilities, technical assumptions and client dependencies.

Managed CMS and site builders

WordPress / WP Essential: content-led business sites, custom themes, plugins and commerce through WooCommerce where suitable. Webflow and Wix: hosted visual CMS/site-builder experiences when editing needs, integrations and platform boundaries fit the brief.

Commerce platforms

Shopify: hosted storefronts, themes, extensions and supported API integrations. WooCommerce: WordPress-based commerce with configurable extensions. OpenCart: an open-source store option for catalog and order requirements that fit its extension and hosting model.

Custom PHP applications

Laravel and Symfony can support tailored business applications, APIs, back-office systems and portals. We scope authentication, roles, data handling, operational workflows, integrations, deployment and maintenance separately.

JavaScript and modern application stacks

React, Vue.js and Angular can support interactive interfaces; Next.js and Nuxt.js add framework options around their ecosystems. Node.js, Express.js and NestJS can be scoped for server-side APIs and services. Selection depends on product complexity, rendering and data needs, team handover, integrations and expected evolution.

MEAN and MERN are stack combinations.

MEAN generally refers to MongoDB, Express, Angular and Node.js; MERN to MongoDB, Express, React and Node.js. These are not one-click platforms. We use such combinations when the product benefits from the selected frontend, server, API and document-database model; relational databases such as PostgreSQL or MySQL may be a better fit for other data needs.

Engineering beyond page build

Cover the experience, integrations and operation around the site.

Web delivery can include connected design, content workflows and technical operations. The items below can be combined where the project needs them; each remains visible in the written scope.

UX/UI and responsive design

Map navigation and user journeys, produce page or screen wireframes, define reusable components and build responsive layouts for the agreed devices. Accessibility criteria, languages, browser support and content responsibility are documented before delivery.

Backend services and integration

Design and connect APIs, identity and roles, forms, notifications, search, data models and approved external services. Backend options may include Laravel, Symfony, Django, Ruby on Rails, Node.js with Express or NestJS, and other scoped technologies; integration behavior, error states and vendor costs are identified.

Performance, migration and care

Audit existing structure, content, dependencies, responsive behavior and hosting constraints; plan migration, redirects and backups; test key paths; then scope maintenance, updates, monitoring and feature work around the platform and service window.

AI-enabled web features

Where the problem justifies it, scope functions such as assisted search, content classification, support experiences or recommendations. Data access, human review, failure handling, model/vendor usage and ongoing operating cost must be agreed. See VSN AI and automation scope →

Commerce beyond a theme

Make the shopping journey and store operations work together.

A usable store depends on accurate product information, clear buying steps and reliable handoffs to the people and systems fulfilling orders.

Catalog and product experience

Plan category structure, collections, attributes, variants, search, filters, product details, media and merchandising so shoppers can find and compare what they need. Product facts, pricing, inventory rules and licensed assets require client approval.

Cart, checkout and payments

Configure the agreed purchase path, shipping and tax settings, order notifications and payment-provider integration where supported. Merchant onboarding, provider eligibility, fees, settlement, fraud review and legal product policies remain with the merchant and third parties.

Store connections

Scope integrations with inventory, accounting, CRM, ERP, point of sale, shipping, fulfillment, marketing or analytics tools. Each connection should identify data ownership, sync timing, permissions, error handling, vendor costs and who resolves failures.

Migration and redesign

Plan content and data mapping for products, categories, media, customer records, orders, URLs, metadata and integrations. Staging imports, backups, redirect maps and sample checks reduce migration risk, but cannot remove every third-party or data-quality risk.

Full-stack scope

Define what happens behind each screen.

For custom web applications, the scope can cover responsive frontend screens, server-side logic, database design, APIs, authentication and role permissions, admin workflows, notifications, reporting and connections to approved external services.

Where useful, the architecture may include server-rendered or single-page interfaces, REST or GraphQL APIs, relational or document databases, background jobs, file handling, search, caching and audit logs. These components are selected to meet explicit requirements; they are not added by default.

Security, privacy, accessibility, performance, backups and monitoring need project-specific acceptance criteria. The proposal identifies what VSN implements, what the hosting or platform provider supplies and what the client must own or configure.

Examples of project fit

  • WordPress or Webflow for editable marketing content with a manageable page model.
  • Shopify or WooCommerce for catalog and order operations aligned with their commerce ecosystems.
  • Laravel or Symfony for custom business rules, internal tools or role-based portals.
  • React, Vue or Angular with Node.js APIs for interactive products where a separated frontend and backend are appropriate.
  • MEAN or MERN when the end-to-end JavaScript approach and MongoDB data model fit the product.

These are starting points for evaluation, not a commitment that one stack suits every project.

How delivery works

A documented path from brief to handover.

Milestones, acceptance checks, client inputs and change handling are agreed before implementation so the build can be reviewed in manageable steps.

01 · Discover

Understand the users and job

Review goals, audiences, current site or process, content, competitor patterns, business rules, platform constraints, integrations and success measures. The output is a prioritized brief and open-questions list.

02 · Define

Map pages, flows and architecture

Agree the sitemap or application journeys, key screens, data needs, roles, integrations, technical approach, content responsibilities, delivery phases and acceptance criteria.

03 · Design and build

Review real increments

Design responsive layouts and reusable components, then implement the scoped pages, CMS structures, features, APIs and connections. Client feedback is scheduled against agreed review checkpoints.

04 · Verify

Test the important paths

Check agreed desktop and mobile views, links, forms, permissions, product/cart/checkout paths, supported browsers, content, integrations and error states. Fixes are prioritized against the written scope.

05 · Launch or transfer

Prepare access and ownership

Confirm domain, hosting or platform access, backups where applicable, redirects, analytics access, environment settings, administrator accounts and rollback responsibilities before a client-approved launch.

06 · Support

Plan what happens after release

Optional maintenance can cover agreed updates, monitoring, issue response, small improvements or feature work. Service windows, response targets, exclusions and recurring fees are defined in a separate support scope.

Quality and ownership

Leave the client able to run what was built.

Each engagement should state who owns the domain, hosting, source repository, CMS or store account, paid apps, API credentials, content and third-party subscriptions. Client-owned accounts and least-privilege access are preferred where practical.

The handover may include administrator access, deployment or publishing steps, agreed technical notes, CMS guidance, known limitations and the list of third-party costs. Source code transfer, licences, app subscriptions, hosting and post-launch support depend on the signed scope and vendor terms.

Scope and outcome limits

Timelines depend on content readiness, approvals, integrations, platform access, testing and change requests. Search ranking, traffic, sales, payment approval, third-party uptime and uninterrupted migration are not guaranteed. Ongoing SEO, copywriting, product photography, hosting, licences, ad spend and data cleanup are included only when the proposal says so.

Common questions

Questions about web development and e-commerce.

Platform, delivery and support details are confirmed in the written proposal before work begins.

Which technologies and platforms can VSN work with?

Depending on the requirements, projects can be scoped around WordPress, WooCommerce, Shopify, Webflow, Wix, OpenCart, Laravel, Symfony, React, Vue.js, Angular or Node.js-based stacks such as MEAN and MERN. The proposal confirms the selected technologies and who will maintain them.

How do you decide between Shopify, WooCommerce or custom e-commerce?

We compare catalog and order needs, required integrations, customization, ownership, editing workflow, operating capacity, recurring platform costs and expected growth. The recommendation follows the project constraints and is documented with trade-offs.

Can you build a full-stack website or web application?

Yes, custom scope can cover the browser interface, server-side application, data model, APIs, roles and approved integrations. Hosting, monitoring, backups, security criteria and maintenance are separately identified in the scope.

Can an existing store or website be redesigned or migrated?

Yes. The migration plan can identify content and data to move, URL redirects, integrations, backups, staging checks and client sign-off. Data quality, third-party systems and platform limits mean that a migration cannot be guaranteed to be risk-free.

Do you include payment, shipping, CRM or ERP integrations?

Integrations can be included when technically supported and agreed. Each one is scoped for the data exchanged, provider access, fees, error handling, testing and responsibilities. Vendor approval, account charges and service availability remain controlled by the vendor.

Do you provide hosting, SEO, content or maintenance?

These can be scoped as project work or separate ongoing services. The proposal states whether hosting, licences, copywriting, product data, technical SEO, analytics setup, updates and support are included and identifies recurring or third-party costs.

How much does a web or e-commerce project cost, and how long does it take?

Price and timeline depend on the platform, page or feature count, custom design, data migration, integrations, content readiness, review cycles and testing. After discovery, VSN can propose a phased scope with assumptions, dependencies and milestone estimates.

Can you add AI features to a web product?

AI-supported search, classification, recommendations or support functions can be scoped when they solve a defined user need. Data access, human review, error handling, privacy expectations, model usage and recurring costs need to be agreed; see AI and automation services.

Who owns the website, store account and source code?

Ownership, account access, source delivery, third-party licences and any usage restrictions are defined in the written agreement. We identify client-provided accounts and dependencies before development and prepare the agreed handover materials at completion.
