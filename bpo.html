Engagement models

A scope that fits the work—not a one-size package.

Fees and staffing depend on task complexity, volume, coverage hours, languages, systems and client dependencies. VSN confirms scope and commercial terms in a written proposal.

Scoped pilot

Start with a bounded workflow, agreed sample volume, review period and success measures to assess fit before expanding.

Dedicated capacity

Reserve defined roles or hours for recurring work, with responsibilities, supervision, leave cover and client-side support clarified.

Managed process

Assign an agreed process to VSN with documented inputs, outputs, quality checks, escalation ownership and reporting cadence.

No staffing level, round-the-clock coverage, response target or business result is assumed unless it is written into the approved scope.

How delivery works

A controlled handover from discovery to review.

Before recurring work starts, both teams establish what “done” means, who can decide, and how exceptions are handled.

Map the workflow

Review tasks, volumes, peak periods, customer types, current tools and known failure points.

Agree the scope

Document roles, service hours, inputs, outputs, exclusions, service measures and escalation owners.

Prepare the team

Set up approved access, work instructions, response guidance, training materials and quality checks.

Run a controlled start

Begin with an agreed pilot or limited workload; review exceptions and adjust instructions before scaling.

Report the work

Share the agreed operational measures, issue trends, quality findings and decisions needed from each side.

Review and improve

Use review meetings to update procedures, training and capacity when the workload or business needs change.

Quality and visibility

Agree how performance will be seen.

Depending on the workflow, reporting can include handled volume, response or resolution time, backlog, quality-review results, processing accuracy, escalations and customer feedback where a reliable source is available.

Definitions, reporting windows, targets and exclusions belong in the statement of work. Results depend on agreed staffing, demand, client systems, source data and timely decisions.

Access and data controls

Access is limited to approved systems and roles. The parties define account ownership, identity checks, permitted data use, incident contacts, retention needs and access removal before work begins.

  • Least-privilege access and MFA where supported
  • Documented escalation and incident route
  • Confidentiality and processing terms when required
  • Client approval for scripts, decisions and customer commitments
Decision rights

Make clear which actions need your approval.

Customer-facing and financial work should have explicit guardrails. The operating guide sets what a team can do independently and what must pause for an authorized client owner.

Routine actions

Team members can use approved answers, categorize requests, gather missing details, update permitted records and complete documented steps within the granted access and agreed limits.

Approval checkpoints

Exceptions such as refunds beyond a stated limit, disputed orders, account ownership changes or requests outside policy are routed to a named client approver before a commitment is made.

Reserved decisions

Policy changes, legal or tax interpretations, unusual financial adjustments, security incidents and high-impact customer remedies remain with the client or the qualified authority they designate.

The examples are starting points only. The final authority matrix, monetary limits, recordkeeping and escalation window are tailored to the process and written into the scope.

Knowledge operations

Keep the working instructions current.

Support quality can drift when product details, promotions or internal procedures change. A maintained source of truth helps the team follow the approved version and helps supervisors spot gaps before they become repeat contacts.

  • Name the client owner who approves content and policy updates
  • Keep a revision date and change note for active procedures
  • Mark temporary promotions and known issues with an expiry or review point
  • Remove obsolete guidance from agent-facing materials
  • Use a small set of approved examples to check the team understands material changes

A practical update route

The client identifies the source material and authorized approver. VSN can format the approved change into workflow instructions, flag conflicting versions and apply it after the owner confirms release. If guidance is missing or inconsistent, the team routes the case instead of improvising policy.

Any knowledge-base platform, content migration, translation or ongoing editorial ownership is included only when the written proposal covers it.

Common questions

Plan the handover with fewer surprises.

Every engagement is scoped to the client’s actual workflow and operating requirements.

How will agents learn our product and brand voice?

The client provides approved product information, policies, examples and escalation contacts. VSN uses these materials to prepare work instructions and training; both sides review sample work before the agreed rollout.

How is customer or business data protected?

Before access, both sides define the data involved, approved systems, minimum required permissions, handling instructions, incident contacts and any contractual data-processing terms required for that workflow.

Can VSN provide 24/7 coverage or a guaranteed response time?

Coverage and response targets depend on staffing, demand, holidays, client systems and the agreed operating model. They are confirmed only when feasible and included in the written scope.

How do we monitor quality and service levels?

The statement of work sets the measures, calculation method, review period and reporting format. Measures may include volume, response time, quality samples, accuracy, backlog and escalations where relevant.

What do we need to provide before work starts?

Typically, the client provides documented procedures, approved scripts or policies, access approvals, training content, escalation owners, expected volumes and timely decisions. Exact dependencies are listed in the project scope.

How is pricing determined, and are there extra costs?

Pricing is proposed after reviewing workload, complexity, coverage, languages, systems and required supervision. Third-party software, telephony, licences, advertising and other pass-through costs are identified separately in the proposal.

What happens if the workload changes or service quality falls?

The review and escalation process is agreed in advance. Material changes to workload, coverage, process or targets may require a revised scope; corrective actions and any exit terms follow the signed agreement.
