Add the capability your team needs, when the work needs it.

Resource Augmentation helps you extend an existing team with individual specialists. For broader delivery needs, VSN can scope a dedicated cross-functional pod with agreed coordination, working cadence and responsibilities.

Engagement models

Choose who leads the day-to-day work.

Scope defines who assigns tasks, manages delivery, reviews work and provides tools. Roles, availability, working overlap, fees and any service levels are agreed before kickoff.

Individual specialist augmentation

Add a specific engineer, designer, QA specialist or other agreed role to your existing team. Your manager prioritizes and directs their day-to-day tasks.

  • Best when your backlog and technical lead are already in place
  • Capacity and expected overlap stated in the engagement
  • Client retains product decisions and acceptance

Dedicated cross-functional pod

Combine complementary roles around a defined product area or workstream. Coordination, planning and progress visibility can be included in the written model.

  • Possible product, design, engineering and QA mix
  • Working cadence and decision rights agreed at kickoff
  • Scope may be capacity-led or outcome-led as documented

Short-term skill or delivery burst

Bring in temporary capacity for a migration, release, backlog spike, platform transition or targeted technical task, subject to availability and fit.

  • Clear start, expected duration and handover goals
  • Useful for a bounded workload or specialist gap
  • Extension or offboarding handled through agreed notice

Fractional specialist support

Scope limited weekly or project-based participation for an experienced specialist when continuous full-time capacity is not required.

  • Product, architecture, UX, QA or delivery support where available
  • Specific hours, response expectations and meeting rhythm
  • Not a substitute for an always-on role unless contracted

How the engagement works

A clear path from role definition to handover.

  1. Define the gap: agree tasks, skill level, tools, location/time-zone needs and success signals.
  2. Confirm fit and availability: review suitable profiles or a proposed team shape; no start date is promised until confirmed.
  3. Set working rules: establish reporting line, communication, access, security, IP, timesheets and acceptance responsibilities.
  4. Onboard with context: provide approved documentation, tools and a named point of contact.
  5. Review capacity and fit: use an agreed check-in and issue escalation path; change scope through written agreement.
  6. Extend or offboard: plan renewals, access removal, knowledge transfer and final records in line with contract terms.
Client and provider responsibilities

For individual augmentation, the client ordinarily owns product direction, task priority, access approval, system decisions and acceptance. In a managed pod, VSN may coordinate the agreed work cadence, but scope boundaries and client approvals remain explicit.

Confidentiality, intellectual property, data handling, equipment, work location, working hours, holidays, substitution or replacement, notice and payment terms are defined in the signed agreement. No person is given access before authorization and onboarding requirements are set.

FAQs

Resource Augmentation questions

Final availability and terms depend on the role, location, engagement model and written agreement.

What is the difference between augmentation and a dedicated team?

With individual augmentation, a specialist joins your existing team and your leads direct daily work. A dedicated pod groups roles around a workstream and may include agreed coordination. The SOW states who manages delivery.

Which roles can we request?

Commonly scoped areas include software, web, mobile, product, UX/UI, QA, AI/data, DevOps, digital, and selected operations roles. Availability and experience are confirmed for each request.

Can we choose the hours and time-zone overlap?

We can discuss location, working hours and overlap needs. The agreed schedule, holidays, response expectations and any out-of-hours work are written into the engagement before start.

How are rates and contract length set?

Fees depend on role, level, location, time commitment and term. The proposal records billing basis, invoicing cadence, expenses and notice. There is no fixed price until requirements are scoped.

Who manages and reviews the work?

The model determines that. Individual resources normally report to your team lead; a managed pod can include specified coordination. Task ownership, acceptance and escalation are documented at kickoff.

How do you handle confidentiality, IP and access?

Confidentiality, ownership, data access, security requirements and offboarding are set in the signed contract and onboarding process. Share only approved access and the minimum information required.

What happens if availability or role fit changes?

We discuss fit and continuity early and record any change, substitution, notice or transition commitments in the contract. Replacement is not assumed unless the agreement explicitly covers it.

Can a team work on our project outcomes rather than capacity?

Yes, where scope and acceptance criteria can be defined, a project or managed delivery SOW may be more suitable than time-based augmentation. We will clarify ownership, dependencies and change control.
