Commercial model

Different work needs different billing.

Fixed-scope products can use simple packages. Complex engineering, BPO and advisory work should use a proposal or SOW rather than forcing every client through the same checkout.

Productized services

Authority profiles and tightly scoped website packages can use published starting prices and clear inclusions.

Custom projects

Apps, SaaS, e-commerce and automation are normally milestone-based after discovery and written scope.

Recurring services

Social media, maintenance, BPO and managed support are normally monthly or quarterly retainers tied to capacity and service levels.

Project control

No surprise scope.

A strong engagement separates agreed deliverables from new requests. Significant changes should be documented before they affect cost or delivery.

Every SOW should define

  • Deliverables and exclusions
  • Milestones and acceptance criteria
  • Client dependencies
  • Revision limits
  • Security and access responsibilities
  • Payment schedule
  • IP ownership and handover
  • Support / warranty period

Review points

Decisions stay visible as work moves forward.

Projects lose time when feedback arrives after the underlying decision has been built into every screen or workflow. We agree which outputs need your sign-off and who has authority to approve them.

Typical checkpoints

  1. Scope: confirm the outcome, exclusions and dependencies.
  2. Direction: review journeys, content and technical approach.
  3. Working build: test the agreed core flows and record changes.
  4. Handover: review access, documentation and unresolved items.

Frequency and acceptance criteria vary by engagement and are written into the proposal or SOW.
