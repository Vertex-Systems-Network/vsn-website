Mobile apps planned for real users, real workflows and the markets they serve.

Mobile application services

Build the product behind the app icon.

A mobile product is more than screens. It may need a clear user journey, a stable app architecture, APIs, account and content management, integrations, support workflows and a plan for how it will be funded and maintained.

iOS app development

Plan and build iPhone and iPad experiences with platform-appropriate navigation, permissions, notification behavior, accessibility and release preparation. Native Swift or SwiftUI can be considered when platform-specific interaction or system capability is important.

Android app development

Design and build Android apps around device compatibility, screen sizes, network conditions and the user journeys relevant to the audience. Kotlin or Java may fit native requirements; the supported device and OS range is agreed at the start.

Cross-platform applications

Flutter with Dart or React Native with JavaScript/TypeScript can share substantial application logic across iOS and Android. We evaluate native modules, device features, accessibility, release workflow and long-term team ownership before choosing.

Mobile companion and web experiences

Build a customer app alongside a responsive web product, partner portal or admin dashboard where different users need different workflows. Shared APIs and identity can connect the experiences while each interface remains suited to its use.

Learning and earning products

Connect skill-building to real opportunities—with clear rules.

We can plan platforms that help people learn, teach, create or access paid work. The app explains the activity and its terms; actual earnings, employment and outcomes depend on demand, eligibility and the platform operator.

Learning and education apps

Course and lesson catalogues, video or audio learning, live-class access, quizzes, assignments, progress tracking, certificates, educator tools, moderation and student support, age-appropriate access and consent needs where relevant. The scope can include multilingual content, lower-bandwidth use, saved progress and offline access where technically suitable.

Learning plus creator or tutor marketplace

Support instructor profiles, course publishing, learner enrolment, reviews, subscriptions or course purchases, creator dashboards, content moderation and transparent revenue-share calculations. Roles, content rights, refund handling and payout reconciliation are defined with the platform owner.

Task, freelance and service marketplaces

Plan listings, search and matching, applications or bookings, messaging, work status, dispute workflows, reviews and administrative oversight. If compensation is involved, eligibility, fees, cancellation, verification and payout rules need to be explicit and reviewed for the target markets.

Rewards and participation features

Where appropriate, implement points, verified milestones, referrals or incentives with documented eligibility, abuse controls, expiry and redemption conditions. Reward points, cash-equivalent balances and withdrawable earnings are different product models and should never be presented as interchangeable.

No app can promise that users will earn money.

VSN can build the software and transparent earning mechanics specified by the product owner. We do not guarantee user income, jobs, investment returns, downloads or platform approval. The owner is responsible for substantiating claims, funding rewards, defining marketplace rules and obtaining legal, tax and payment advice for each target market.

Product capabilities

Plan user journeys and the systems behind them.

Feature selection follows the product’s users, launch market, operating team and measurable first release. Optional functions such as payments, location, camera or offline access are added only with a clear need.

Accounts and roles

Registration, sign-in, profile management, onboarding and role-based access for learners, instructors, buyers, sellers, field teams or administrators. Account recovery and moderation flows are included when agreed.

Commerce and subscriptions

Product or course catalogues, cart or enrolment, membership access, receipts and payment-provider integrations where available. App-store rules for digital content and subscriptions vary by storefront, product and region; current requirements, provider eligibility, fees and payout arrangements must be checked before implementation.

Backend, APIs and admin tools

Connect mobile clients to approved APIs, databases, content management, reporting, customer support, CRM or existing business software. Admin tools can manage content, users, activity, support cases and the settings the client needs to operate.

Notifications and device features

Scope push notifications, deep links, camera or file access, location, maps and device biometrics where relevant. Permissions, consent, notification preferences, data retention and graceful fallbacks are part of the acceptance plan.

International and local readiness

Plan language, right-to-left layouts where needed, date and time formats, currencies, phone formats, region-specific content and low-bandwidth behavior. Target countries, store accounts, payment options and any local compliance requirements are confirmed before build decisions.

Analytics and product learning

Define a small set of product events around activation, lesson completion, checkout, booking or retention, then agree privacy-aware analytics and reporting. Events should answer a product question, not collect more personal data than the workflow needs.

Technology choices

Choose native or cross-platform based on constraints.

Swift / SwiftUI and Kotlin / Java support dedicated Apple and Android implementations. Flutter / Dart and React Native / TypeScript can share code across platforms while still needing platform-specific testing and integrations. Progressive web apps may suit browser-first access and simpler distribution, but do not automatically replace native app capabilities or store needs.

For backend services, the project may use an existing client system or a scoped API and data layer built with technologies such as Node.js, Laravel, Symfony or another fit. Hosting, monitoring, file storage, notifications and third-party services are confirmed as dependencies rather than assumed to be included.

The trade-offs we review

  • Which device features and platform-specific interactions are essential?
  • Do performance, offline use or release frequency change the architecture?
  • Who will maintain the code and manage store accounts after handover?
  • What do the API, backend, admin, content and support workflows need?
  • Which vendor fees, developer accounts and external services recur?

Delivery lifecycle

Move from product question to tested release.

Milestones include review points and client inputs so the team can validate the important decisions before the full build is committed.

01 · Discover

Understand users and product value

Clarify the audience, problem, current alternatives, operating model, target regions, required integrations, monetization assumptions and measurable first release. The result is a brief, initial roadmap and unresolved-risk list.

02 · Define

Prioritize an MVP and acceptance criteria

Separate launch-critical workflows from later features. Agree user roles, stories, data flows, supported devices, constraints, content responsibility, milestone reviews and what must pass before release.

03 · Design

Prototype the key journeys

Map navigation and task flows, test wireframes with stakeholders, then create the agreed visual system and screen specifications. Design feedback is captured before implementation advances.

04 · Build

Deliver increments with product feedback

Implement mobile screens, backend connections and admin tools in planned increments. Client-owned developer accounts, test credentials, content and timely decisions are listed as project dependencies.

05 · Verify

Test real devices and release paths

Test target iOS and Android versions, screen sizes, network conditions, accessibility basics, permissions, key user journeys, error handling, API behavior and privacy disclosures according to the agreed matrix.

06 · Release and support

Prepare submission and continued care

Prepare agreed store listing assets, release notes, builds and submission steps. Store review decisions remain with Apple or Google. Optional post-launch support can cover crash triage, OS compatibility, security updates, bug fixes and scoped feature releases.

Ownership and operations

Plan who runs the service after launch.

Agree ownership and access for the source repository, Apple and Google developer accounts, backend hosting, analytics, domains, API keys, payment or messaging providers and app-store assets. Client-controlled accounts and least-privilege access are preferred where practical.

The handover can include release instructions, environment notes, app configuration, admin guidance, known limitations and a maintenance proposal. Developer account fees, cloud costs, paid SDKs, messaging charges and support retainers are identified separately.

App-store and payment boundaries

App review, billing options and digital-content payment rules can vary by store, country, app type and current platform policy. We identify those dependencies during product planning, but final eligibility and approval are decided by the platform and the client’s appointed advisers.

Common questions

Questions about mobile app development.

Platform and release decisions depend on user needs, selected regions, account eligibility and the accepted project scope.

Can you build learning and earning apps?

Yes. Scope can include learning content, courses, quizzes, progress, tutor tools and a marketplace or task workflow where people may earn for approved work, teaching or sales. User income, job availability and rewards are not guaranteed; product rules and claims must be clear and substantiated by the app owner.

Should we build native apps, Flutter or React Native?

It depends on platform-specific features, user experience, performance, offline needs, development and maintenance resources, release plans and existing skills. We compare these trade-offs with you before proposing a stack; no single option is right for every app.

Can an app connect to an existing website, CRM, LMS or store?

Yes, if the existing service exposes supported access or APIs and the account owner authorizes it. The scope identifies the source of truth, data exchanged, permissions, failure handling, provider fees and testing responsibilities.

Can you include payments, subscriptions or user payouts?

Payment and payout flows can be scoped with a suitable provider and client-owned accounts. Digital goods and subscriptions may follow different app-store billing rules by region and app type; provider eligibility, required verification, taxes, fees and payout terms must be checked for the chosen markets.

Will VSN publish the app to the App Store and Google Play?

We can prepare the agreed release builds, metadata and submission steps when the client provides eligible developer accounts, required disclosures and content. Apple and Google review and decide whether to approve each submission; approval is not guaranteed.

How do you protect users and prevent marketplace abuse?

Depending on the product, controls may include role-based permissions, report and block tools, moderation queues, transaction states, audit logs, abuse limits and escalation procedures. The product owner defines policies and staffing; technical features do not replace legal review or day-to-day moderation.

How much does an app cost and how long will it take?

Cost and timing depend on the number of platforms and user roles, screen and feature scope, backend and admin work, integrations, content, payments, device support and testing. After discovery, VSN can outline a phased MVP with assumptions and milestones.

What happens after launch?

Ongoing work can cover OS compatibility, bug fixes, crash investigation, security updates, feature development and store submission support. Response targets, service window, included hours, exclusions and recurring costs are agreed separately.
